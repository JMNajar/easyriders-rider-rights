The Issue Explained

What "Right to Repair" Means for Motorcycles

Modern motorcycles are increasingly software-driven. Engine management, ABS systems, traction control, ride modes, and now telematics are all controlled by proprietary software that only OEM-authorized dealers can access and modify. When your bike needs service or a diagnostic readout, many riders are finding that their only legal option is the dealer â€” at dealer prices, on dealer timelines.

The Telematics Problem Newer bikes transmit diagnostic data wirelessly. Manufacturers are using this capability to lock out independent shops â€” not because independent shops lack the skill, but because the data stream is encrypted and proprietary. The REPAIR Act would require manufacturers to make this data available to anyone who asks, on reasonable terms.
What Manufacturers Argue OEMs claim that open repair data creates cybersecurity risks and enables intellectual property theft. They also argue that independent shops may not maintain their vehicles to safety standards without OEM oversight. The AMA and MRF dispute both claims, pointing to independent shops' long safety record and the cybersecurity frameworks already used by aftermarket tool manufacturers.
The Massachusetts Precedent Massachusetts voters passed a ballot initiative requiring manufacturers to provide telematics access in 2020. Manufacturers sued, claiming federal preemption. A federal district court upheld the law in 2026 â€” clearing the strongest legal obstacle to state and federal right-to-repair legislation nationwide.
