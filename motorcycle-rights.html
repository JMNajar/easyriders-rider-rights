The Issues Explained

Lane Splitting & Lane Filtering

Lane splitting allows motorcyclists to ride between lanes of moving traffic. Lane filtering allows movement between stopped or very slow traffic. Both reduce rear-end collision risk — the leading cause of motorcycle fatalities in urban environments.

Current Law Only California explicitly permits lane splitting. Utah (2019), Montana (2021), and Hawaii (2018 filtering only) permit filtering under specific conditions. All other states prohibit or are silent on the practice.
The Safety Argument A University of California, Berkeley study found lane splitting in traffic moving at 50 mph or less significantly reduced injury risk. Utah's 2019 data shows zero filtering-related fatalities in the first three years.
What's Moving Texas (HB 1927), Minnesota (HF 1623), Georgia, Tennessee, North Carolina, Virginia, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Ohio, and Florida all have active bills this session. Texas is the closest to passage.

Motorcycle Profiling

Motorcycle profiling occurs when law enforcement targets riders for traffic stops, searches, or surveillance based solely on their motorcycle affiliation — the type of bike, the clothing worn, or the patches displayed — rather than observed traffic violations or criminal activity.

The Federal Bill The Stop Motorcycle Profiling Act (S.1424 / H.R.2843) would direct the Department of Justice to issue guidance requiring state and local law enforcement to adopt written policies prohibiting profiling of motorcyclists. Washington State passed similar legislation at the state level in 2011 — the first in the nation.
State Progress Washington, Oregon, and several other states have enacted profiling protections. The MRF's state-by-state progress map shows 17 states with some form of anti-profiling policy as of 2026.

HOV Lane Access

High-Occupancy Vehicle lanes were designed to reduce traffic by incentivizing carpooling. Motorcycles — which are inherently space-efficient and reduce congestion — are permitted in HOV lanes in most states, but inconsistencies create confusion and enforcement problems.

Where Riders Stand Most states permit motorcycles in HOV lanes regardless of occupancy. However, several states and some federally-funded HOV projects have created restrictions. The AMA tracks HOV access on a state-by-state basis.
